## Design Context

### Users
Two primary audiences: (1) academics — peers, collaborators, potential advisors, conference reviewers who want to assess research depth and breadth; (2) industry/research internship recruiters who need to quickly judge technical range and design sensibility. Both arrive with high context on HCI/AI and will read critically rather than skim.

### Brand Personality
**Bold, technical, inventive.** The portfolio should read like the work it showcases — rigorous but imaginative, systems-minded but human-centered. Confidence without excess. The person behind the work does healthcare AI, data visualization, and game design — that range should feel coherent, not scattered.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Dark mode only, intentional
- **Accent**: Orange (#fb923c / orange-400) — warm, distinctive, less clichéd than cyan/purple AI-portfolio defaults
- **Font**: Bai Jamjuree (weights 300/400/600) — geometric, clean, slightly technical
- **Anti-aesthetic**: No glassmorphism, no gradient text, no hero metrics, no identical card grids — the work is the content, not the chrome
- **Tone**: Editorial precision over decorative flair. A design researcher's portfolio should itself demonstrate design judgment.
- **References**: Not specified — approach as a clean slate with the bold/technical/inventive constraint

### Design Principles
1. **Work over chrome**: The content (projects, publications, experience) must breathe — no decorative noise competing for attention
2. **Earned distinctiveness**: Every unusual choice should serve legibility or personality, not just be different for its own sake
3. **AAA accessibility**: WCAG AAA is the target — contrast, semantics, and keyboard nav are non-negotiable
4. **Coherent range**: The portfolio spans healthcare AI, visualization, game design, and industry UX — the design system should hold all of these without feeling fragmented
5. **Respect expert readers**: Both audiences are sophisticated — write and design for scanning, not convincing
